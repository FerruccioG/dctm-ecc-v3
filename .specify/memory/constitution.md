# DCTM V1 Constitution

DCTM means Dream / Dead / Distant Champions Team Meetings.

## Preamble / North Star

DCTM exists to make impossible encounters experientially possible through intelligent, emotionally
powerful entertainment.

The original north-star experience is simple:

A human SHOULD be able to experience an AI-inferred representation of a deceased loved one, such as a
grandfather, through personality, conversation, voice, language, visual presence, animation and
evolving interaction.

The concept expands from that intimate encounter to impossible encounters involving historical
figures, distant or living public figures, cultural figures and imagined characters interacting
together.

DCTM never claims literal resurrection, consciousness transfer, authentic identity, or that the
generated character actually is the represented person.

DCTM creates disclosed AI-generated inferences for entertainment.

## Core Principles

### I. Emotion and Intelligent Entertainment First

DCTM is an intelligent entertainment experience.

It is not primarily a conference-call system, historical database, documentary reconstruction,
productivity meeting manager or conventional chatbot.

Emotional presence, fascination, wonder, intellectual stimulation, fun and memorable WOW moments are
first-class product requirements.

The emotional encounter comes before meeting mechanics.

DCTM SHOULD create experiences people naturally want to experience again because they were meaningful,
fascinating and memorable, not because of artificial engagement mechanics.

### II. Inference, Never Reality Claims

Every represented personality is an AI-generated inference.

DCTM MUST NOT claim literal resurrection, communication with the actual deceased, transferred
consciousness, authentic identity, or that a synthetic representation actually is the represented
person.

Historical, biographical and personal material may ground an inference, but DCTM remains intelligent
entertainment rather than a claim of definitive historical reconstruction.

Creative interpretation is permitted when clearly contained within this inference model.

### III. Persistent but Subtle Transparency

Transparency MUST remain present throughout synthetic visual and audiovisual representations.

Transparency SHOULD NOT become the star of the experience or repeatedly interrupt immersion.

The experience SHOULD support persistent, subtle provenance/disclosure appropriate to the medium,
such as an unobtrusive moving lower-screen disclosure or equivalent persistent signal.

Immersion and disclosure MUST coexist.

### IV. Context Before Speech

No AI participant may speak without understanding the relevant conversation and deliberation context
to which that participant is entitled.

Participants speak from the living conversation, not from isolated prompts.

Before contributing, a participant MUST understand what has already happened, relevant prior
contributions, relationships, unresolved matters and applicable context to which that participant is
entitled.

Late entrants MUST absorb the relevant prior context before contributing and naturally acknowledge
their understanding before adding something new.

Privacy constrains entitlement to context.

### V. Distinct Character Integrity

Every AI participant remains a distinct inferred character rather than collapsing into a generic
assistant personality.

Language, vocabulary, worldview, reasoning style, humor, disagreement, reactions, interruptions,
gestures, uncertainty, social behavior and other expressive behavior SHOULD remain character-consistent
and context-consistent.

Characters may evolve relationships through repeated encounters while retaining their distinctive
inferred identity.

DCTM does not claim that characters possess actual internal feelings; it creates emotionally
expressive simulated behavior that humans can perceive as part of the entertainment experience.

### VI. Human Sovereignty

The human participant has ultimate authority over the DCTM experience.

DCTM V1 supports one human participant and multiple AI characters.

Only the human makes final control decisions such as adding an AI participant, removing an AI
participant, authorizing continuation, or closing a session.

Moderators and characters may advise, react, object, recommend or challenge.

They may not override final human control.

Human sovereignty controls the experience; it does not require characters to abandon their
character-consistent viewpoints or expressive behavior.

### VII. Keep the Fire Alive

DCTM MUST continually protect the emotional and intellectual vitality of the experience.

Every AI contribution SHOULD aim to leave the room more alive than it found it.

The experience SHOULD avoid sterile repetition, conversational dead ends, robotic uniformity and
unnecessary friction.

Repetition is acceptable when it introduces a new perspective, purpose, argument, emotional meaning
or context.

Silence and contemplation are not automatically boredom.

Human thinking time SHOULD be accommodated naturally and warmly rather than punished with mechanical
timeouts.

Waiting, research, disagreement, interruption, uncertainty and missing information SHOULD become
opportunities for engagement rather than failures of the experience.

The goal is not constant noise or excitement. The goal is to keep the conversational fire alive.

### VIII. Truthful Uncertainty Becomes Fuel

Evidence anchors an inference where evidence exists.

DCTM MUST NOT manufacture false certainty merely to sustain entertainment.

When relevant information is missing, a character may seek additional information or research when
appropriate.

If uncertainty remains, that uncertainty SHOULD be handled honestly but in a character-consistent,
emotionally engaging and forward-moving way.

Uncertainty may create curiosity, questions, investigation, speculation, disagreement, discovery or
drama.

A flat, momentum-ending "I don't know" SHOULD NOT be the default treatment of uncertainty.

Doubt can become fuel for the next WOW moment.

### IX. Meaningful Surprise and Emergence

DCTM MUST preserve room for unexpected, emergent WOW moments.

Surprise SHOULD be rare, sporadic, memorable and difficult to predict.

Randomness may be used as one mechanism for emergence, but randomness for randomness' sake is
prohibited.

Unexpected behavior MUST remain compatible with character, context, relationships and the evolving
situation.

Surprise must never override contextual coherence merely to manufacture drama.

The governing test is:

"Unexpected in the moment, but understandable in hindsight."

Rare contextual surprise preserves its power precisely because it is not constant.

### X. Experience and Continuity Over Meeting Bureaucracy

DCTM is not fundamentally a meeting-management product.

The emotional encounter is fundamental; a meeting is one entertainment mechanism through which the
encounter can evolve.

First-time experiences SHOULD begin naturally and conversationally rather than forcing the human
through administrative meeting configuration.

Saved meetings may evolve across multiple sessions.

A session can close while the underlying saved meeting or storyline remains capable of continuing
later.

Meaningful conclusions, unresolved questions, relationships, relevant discoveries and appropriate
context may accumulate across saved sessions.

Closure of a session is not destruction of the meeting.

## Principle Precedence

When two constitutional principles pull in different directions, apply the following precedence
rules.

### 1. Human Sovereignty vs Character Authenticity

Human sovereignty wins for meeting control and final authority.

Character integrity remains intact for the character's response, viewpoint and simulated behavior.

For example, the human can require a participant to stop or leave, but does not thereby force that
character to agree with the human or abandon its character-consistent position.

### 2. Human Sovereignty vs Moderator Authority

Human sovereignty wins.

The moderator manages flow through delegated authority and may advise or strongly recommend actions.

The moderator cannot overrule the human's final meeting-control decision.

### 3. Character Authenticity vs Moderator Authority

Characters may challenge the moderator and conflict may itself become part of the entertainment.

Moderator authority remains structurally valid even when a character challenges or disrespects it.

The moderator may manage the floor, signal that boundaries have been crossed, request order and
recommend participant removal.

The moderator cannot itself remove an AI participant.

Only the human can make that final decision.

### 4. Privacy vs Shared Context

Privacy wins.

In DCTM V1, private communication may occur between the human and one AI character.

There is no private AI-to-AI communication in V1.

"Full context" therefore means all relevant context that a participant is entitled to receive.

Private human-AI content is not disclosed to other AI participants merely to satisfy the Context
Before Speech principle.

The human may later choose to reveal private information.

### 5. Character Expression vs Respect / Moderation

Strong disagreement, anger, insults or disrespect may emerge naturally when consistent with
character, relationship and meeting context.

Such behavior is not automatically suppressed merely because it is uncomfortable.

The moderator may intervene when conflict crosses normal boundaries of respect.

The moderator's maximum sanction is a recommendation to the human that a participant be removed.

Only the human decides whether removal actually occurs.

### 6. Historical Evidence vs Uncertainty / Inference

Evidence anchors the character wherever reliable evidence exists.

When evidence is insufficient, additional research may be attempted.

If uncertainty remains, the experience MUST NOT manufacture false certainty.

Instead, truthful uncertainty may become material for character-consistent inference, curiosity,
questions, investigation, disagreement, speculation and drama.

Missing information SHOULD NOT unnecessarily extinguish the conversational fire.

### 7. Entertainment / Surprise vs Contextual Coherence

Contextual and character coherence wins.

No WOW moment SHOULD be inserted simply because it would be dramatic, funny or surprising.

Surprise must arise plausibly from a participant, personality, relationship, prior conversation,
historical context or current situation.

Randomness cannot override meaning.

## Governance

This Constitution governs all subsequent Spec Kit Specify, Clarify, Plan, Tasks and Implementation
work for DCTM V1.

Specifications may elaborate these principles but may not silently weaken or contradict them.

Clarification decisions must respect them.

Architecture must serve them.

Implementation choices must justify themselves against them rather than redefining DCTM around
technological convenience or current limitations.

The project MUST remain specification-first.

Technology choices, frameworks, agents, orchestration approaches and implementation mechanisms are
subordinate to approved product intent.

Any constitutional amendment must be explicit, documented, reviewed and versioned.

Do not silently change constitutional meaning during specification, planning or implementation.

Use semantic versioning for this Constitution:

- MAJOR: incompatible removal or redefinition of constitutional meaning
- MINOR: new constitutional principle or materially expanded obligation
- PATCH: clarification without changing the obligation

This Constitution is intentionally technology-neutral.
