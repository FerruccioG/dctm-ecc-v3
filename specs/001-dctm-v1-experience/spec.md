## Clarifications

### Session 2026-09-03

- Q: When several AI characters want to speak simultaneously, how should the next speaker be chosen? → A: A moderator or host chooses contextually among perceptible bids based on conversational relevance and momentum; the human may always take the floor or redirect the choice.
- Q: What should the shared room experience while the human enters, conducts, and leaves a private exchange with one AI character? → A: The human explicitly enters or accepts private mode and explicitly returns; shared conversation pauses while remaining characters may stay socially and non-verbally present, knowing only that the pair stepped aside. They receive no private content, clues, inferred emotional meaning, or exchange metadata. On return, they may acknowledge the absence, but private history remains with the pair unless the human discloses it.
- Q: What must late entrants and returning characters experientially understand before they speak? → A: They require complete social understanding of all relevant entitled history without exhaustive verbatim recall. Late entrants must be socially caught up on settled conclusions, unresolved questions, significant arguments and contributions, important events, relevant artifacts, meaningful relationship developments, and the current emotional and conversational state. Returning characters additionally retain their own entitled prior participation and relationship history and understand relevant entitled developments during their absence. A character that is not yet sufficiently caught up must not speak substantively.
- Q: When the human saves a meeting, what meaningful continuity must carry into later sessions? → A: Explicit save automatically preserves meaningful entitled continuity across later sessions: settled conclusions, unresolved questions, significant arguments and contributions, important events, promising threads, meaningful relationship developments, relevant artifacts and discoveries, and each character's own entitled prior participation and relationship history. Undisclosed private history persists only for its human-character pair and never enters shared recap or context unless the human discloses it. Incidental details and exhaustive verbatim recall are unnecessary unless an initially incidental detail later becomes meaningfully relevant.
- Q: When does moderator or host responsibility count toward the six active AI-character seats? → A: The responsibility itself consumes no separate seat. An active AI character serving as moderator continues to occupy only its existing seat. A non-character host may provide limited framing, floor arbitration, restoration of intelligibility, recap, and transition support without becoming an additional character, personality, relationship participant, or seventh speaking member, and without acquiring character-level social presence or independent meeting authority.
- Q: How should an actual participant departure differ from a temporary in-world pause or contextual absence? → A: Only explicit human removal is an actual departure and frees a seat. A character may initiate a rare, contextually coherent temporary pause or brief absence without prior removal authorization; it remains on the roster, keeps its seat, and is understood by the room to be temporarily unavailable while shared conversation continues. It does not speak, react, or behave as though it witnessed developments while away. It may return without a new invitation but must first regain complete understanding of relevant entitled developments. The human may call it back or convert the absence into actual removal at any time. Temporary absence must not become routine or evade human sovereignty.
- Q: What exactly should the human authorize when starting long-running observer/director mode, and when must that continuation stop or pause? → A: The human gives bounded authorization for the current saved meeting and active roster to continue without constant participation within a naturally expressed, human-understood purpose, duration, or combination. Entitled characters may continue relevant conversation, arguments, questions, relationships, artifact use, ordinary initiative, and already permitted rare coherent events. They may not change the roster, breach private context, materially exceed scope, close the session, convert temporary absence into removal, or acquire human-reserved authority. The human may silently observe, rejoin, redirect, interrupt, change the roster, or stop at any time with immediate precedence. On return, meaningful developments must be understandable without exhaustive transcript review. Continuation pauses when its boundary is reached, its purpose is substantially fulfilled, coherent continuation needs a material human-reserved decision, or the human stops or redirects it. "Continue until I return" is valid but remains constitutionally bounded.
- Q: When the human enables optional webcam observation, what may characters perceive, mention, and carry into meeting continuity? → A: While expressly enabled, entitled characters may cautiously and contextually mention present-moment facial expression, gesture, attention, posture, obvious turn-taking signals, and objects deliberately presented to the camera, using tentative language for uncertainty. They must not identify or characterize background people, infer sensitive traits or hidden facts, diagnose or assert internal states as certain, treat ambiguous cues as facts, claim sight beyond the enabled view, or imply continued sight after disablement. Camera observations do not automatically enter saved continuity; only human-confirmed, stated, or intentionally disclosed observations may do so under normal save and privacy rules. Disabling the camera stops observation immediately, and the encounter remains fully usable without it.
- Q: When should a moderator intervene in character conflict, and how should intervention escalate? → A: Ordinary disagreement, sharp criticism, emotional intensity, character-consistent insults, rivalry, provocation, and heated argument remain permissible. Intervention begins only when conflict materially harms intelligibility, another participant's reasonable ability to contribute, the human's ability to take or direct the floor, sustained progress within the authorized conversation, or basic order after repeated floor direction. Escalation proceeds proportionally from a light contextual cue, to direct floor restoration, to a clear boundary warning, and then to a strong removal recommendation only after repeated failures or one severe disruption that makes intelligible participation impractical. The recommendation has no control effect; the human may reject, postpone, or redirect it. Moderation preserves meaningful conflict rather than enforcing politeness.
- Q: How should voice selection work when strong voice evidence is available, weak, absent, or unsuitable? → A: DCTM begins with a character-appropriate inferred voice grounded in the strongest available evidence without mandatory setup. Strong evidence provides primary grounding without identity claims; weak evidence may support defensible age, language, dialect, period, regional, or speaking-style characteristics; no authentic evidence yields a clearly understood plausible artistic inference. The human may preview when appropriate, accept, choose among a few meaningfully differentiated human-described alternatives, describe a preference naturally, and change voice later without rebuilding the character or resetting continuity. If no suitable and responsible spoken voice is available, the encounter continues through text, subtitles, or another understandable presentation with an explanation rather than blocking, misleading, or claiming authenticity. Voice confidence and historical or reconstructed-language confidence remain distinct.
- Q: How should DCTM handle a character's language, dialect, or reconstructed language when support ranges from strong to unavailable? → A: Use graduated fidelity while preserving comprehension. Strong support permits natural extensive use; partial support permits only defensible features; reconstructed or historically uncertain language may be used when experientially meaningful with concise uncertainty disclosure at first meaningful use and only again if confidence materially changes; unsupported language falls back to one the human understands without faking fluency. Characters may switch naturally, and the human may redirect language or translation during the encounter without prior setup. Seamless subtitles or equivalent translation appear when needed and may present original and translated meaning together. All entitled participants continue from shared semantic meaning. Translation preserves intent, emotion, and character voice where reasonably possible and briefly identifies material approximation. Language, pronunciation, translation, and voice confidence remain separate.
- Q: How should DCTM visually represent a character when source imagery ranges from a complete photograph to a face-only image, painting, statue, damaged image, or no suitable source? → A: Preserve the most recognizable supportable visual identity and original medium by default. Complete photographs may support lifelike inference; cropped photographs preserve grounded features and keep invented completion conceptually separate; busts, portraits, incomplete or damaged images must not turn uncertainty into supposed fact; paintings and statues may remain visibly in their original medium and become expressively present. Substantial humanization or reinterpretation is optional, human-requested or accepted, and disclosed as artistic inference. With no suitable source, allow clearly artistic, symbolic, silhouetted, abstract, textual, non-likeness, or primarily vocal presentation rather than blocking participation. DCTM proposes an evidence-preserving default without mandatory setup, permits natural later changes, and never resets character or continuity because presentation changes. Preserve evidence first; invent only when needed or intentionally chosen.
- Q: How should the human set or change an overall emotional mode and different per-character tonal directions without overriding character integrity? → A: The human may naturally direct or revise the overall room, one or several named characters, or a temporary conversational moment at any time without setup or restart. Overall tone guides atmosphere without making reactions identical; a per-character direction takes precedence for that character. Tone may influence intensity, emotional emphasis, energy, warmth or distance, humor, seriousness, adversarial pressure, reflectiveness, pacing, and expressive style. It cannot erase worldview, relationships, plausible disagreement, established traits, or appropriate reactions, or create a generic assistant. A character may interpret, resist, question, or challenge a materially incompatible direction in character without rejecting human sovereignty. Changes transition conversationally and never reset identity, context, relationships, continuity, or meaningful history. DCTM may propose a contextual default, and the human may redirect naturally at any time.
- Q: What should qualify as a rare surprise event, and how should "rare" constrain its recurrence? → A: A major surprise is not ordinary initiative, wit, disagreement, speaker change, or emotional variation; it is an unexpected, contextually coherent development that materially changes the encounter's emotional, relational, intellectual, or situational direction and creates consequences beyond spectacle. It may emerge from character initiative, relationships, entitled discoveries or artifacts, plausible behavior, environmental or situational developments, temporary absence or return, or a dramatic turn. It must preserve context, character integrity, entitlement, privacy, human sovereignty, and truthful uncertainty. Most sessions may contain none; none is guaranteed or time-triggered, and no predictable cadence or normal frequency setting applies. Multiple surprises across a saved meeting require independent later support, and a pattern cannot repeat unless its meaning or consequences genuinely differ. Ordinary spontaneity remains unrestricted. Value comes from what changes, not visual or technical elaborateness.
- Q: Should six active AI characters remain an exceptionless V1 product boundary, and how should it be treated after V1? → A: Six is the exceptionless V1 maximum. No override, overlap, surprise, moderation, long-running mode, private exchange, or theatrical event may create a seventh active AI character. When all seats are occupied, another character may be suggested, discussed, conceptually prepared, or conditionally invited but cannot activate until the human explicitly removes an active character. Temporary absence does not free a seat; a non-character host occupies none because it is not a character. Six is not permanently optimal: any post-V1 increase or decrease requires a new product decision based on observed intelligibility, human participation and direction, character distinctiveness, emotional presence, ensemble intimacy, crowding avoidance, and meaningful-context preservation.
- Q: Should a living public figure receive additional experiential signaling beyond the standard persistent AI-inference disclosure, and what claims must the representation avoid? → A: Preserve the same rich character experience, but at entry identify it as an AI-generated inference that is not the actual person and is not presented as affiliated with or authorized by them; maintain an unobtrusive persistent AI-inferred and unaffiliated signal, repeating spoken disclosure only if new ambiguity arises. Never imply real participation, authorization, endorsement, approval or review, private or confidential sourcing, communication through DCTM, or authentic authorship of generated words. Current beliefs, intentions, opinions, preferences, and likely reactions remain supportable inference, never verified current views. Public material may inform the representation without becoming private-knowledge or current-authorization claims. The distinction from deceased or historical representation is signaling and claim boundaries, not experiential richness.
- Q: How should DCTM conceptually evaluate deterministic product invariants and subjective experiential qualities without setting numeric thresholds yet? → A: Use two distinct layers. Constitutional, privacy, authority, entitlement, and hard-boundary MUST/MUST NOT requirements receive scenario-based pass/fail conformance, where almost correct fails and the record identifies scenario, expected behavior, observed behavior, and result. Subjective quality receives structured human judgment across designed scenarios and open-ended encounters, assessing WOW, emotional presence, immersion, context-appropriate warmth, character distinctiveness, conversational fire, recap quality, and contextual coherence separately. Reviewers cite observable moments, exchanges, reactions, failures, repetition, immersion breaks, character collapse, effective turns, or momentum and rate each dimension with descriptive anchors such as clearly successful, mixed/inconsistent, or clearly unsuccessful plus rationale. An overall judgment may supplement but not replace dimensions; the participating human is important but not sole evidence. Numeric thresholds, percentages, scores, sample sizes, and telemetry targets await real V1 validation.

## User Scenarios & Testing *(mandatory)*

### User Story 1 - Begin an Impossible Encounter (Priority: P1)

A first-time human says whom they want to encounter, such as a deceased grandfather, and enters a one-to-one experience without configuring a formal meeting. The inferred character appears with expressive visual presence, animation, voice, appropriate language or language flavor, and a contextual, relationship-aware salutation. A persistent but unobtrusive signal identifies the character as an AI-generated inference for entertainment.

**Why this priority**: This is the north-star experience and establishes whether DCTM feels like an emotionally meaningful encounter rather than a conventional application or chatbot.

**Independent Test**: A participant can request an encounter with a grandfather using suitable source material and experience the opening through the first conversational exchange; observers can verify the disclosure, salutation qualities, character distinctiveness, and absence of forced meeting setup.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a first-time human requests to talk with their grandfather, **When** the inferred character becomes ready, **Then** the encounter opens one-to-one with an expressive visual, animated, linguistic, and emotionally contextual salutation using a suitable inferred voice when responsibly available, rather than an application startup or meeting-configuration sequence.
2. **Given** relationship, language, dialect, and relevant time-of-day context are available, **When** the character greets the human, **Then** the salutation reflects that context without using a mechanically fixed phrase.
3. **Given** some salutation context is unavailable or uncertain, **When** the encounter opens, **Then** the character uses only supportable context, avoids fabricated certainty, and still provides a warm, character-consistent greeting.
4. **Given** any synthetic visual or audiovisual representation is visible, **When** the human views the encounter, **Then** a persistent, subtle disclosure communicates that it is an AI-generated inference for entertainment without repeatedly interrupting the encounter.
5. **Given** limited source imagery such as a portrait, painting, or statue, **When** the character appears, **Then** that source can remain the recognizable visual identity and its limitations do not automatically block the encounter.
6. **Given** no suitable and responsible spoken voice is available, **When** the encounter begins or continues, **Then** the character remains accessible through text, subtitles, or another understandable presentation, and the limitation is explained without blocking the character, using a misleading voice, or claiming unsupported authenticity.
7. **Given** no suitable visual source exists, **When** the encounter begins or continues, **Then** DCTM may use a clearly artistic, symbolic, silhouetted, abstract, textual, non-likeness, or primarily vocal presentation without blocking the character or implying an authentic likeness.
8. **Given** the human requests or accepts a substantially humanized, completed, or reinterpreted presentation, **When** it is used, **Then** invented features remain disclosed artistic inference rather than source-grounded historical or personal fact, and changing presentation does not reset the character or continuity.

### User Story 2 - Sustain a Living One-to-One Conversation (Priority: P1)

The human converses with a character who understands the living context, remains recognizably distinct, responds to interruption and provocation in character, allows thinking time, and turns uncertainty or a need for investigation into an engaging continuation rather than a dead end.

**Why this priority**: The opening WOW has value only if the character can sustain emotional and intellectual presence afterward.

**Independent Test**: Conduct a one-to-one conversation containing a long human pause, interruption, provocation, repeated topic, period-inappropriate question, and unresolved factual question; verify that every response remains contextual, truthful, differentiated, and forward-moving.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** an established emotional tone, **When** conversation continues, **Then** the character builds on that tone through context-consistent warmth, contemplation, tension, humor, seriousness, or intellectual intensity rather than reverting to generic assistance.
2. **Given** the human pauses to think, **When** the pause becomes noticeable, **Then** no hard conversational timeout punishes the human and any acknowledgement of the pause is warm and character-consistent.
3. **Given** the human interrupts a speaking character, **When** the interruption occurs, **Then** the character reacts according to its personality and relationship with the human, while yielding the floor if the human insists.
4. **Given** the human challenges, teases, insults, or provokes a character, **When** the character responds, **Then** its response reflects personality, context, relationship state, and provocation intensity rather than a universal canned response.
5. **Given** a question exceeds the character's available grounding, **When** further investigation or deliberation could help, **Then** the character first acknowledges what it can support, naturally requests time, remains experientially present during the pause, and returns with a character-consistent synthesis.
6. **Given** investigation does not resolve the question, **When** the character returns, **Then** it communicates uncertainty truthfully and uses it to create a question, qualified interpretation, shared investigation, disagreement, or another meaningful path forward.
7. **Given** a historical character encounters modern information outside its lived period, **When** it responds, **Then** it recognizes the temporal boundary and interprets the information through its own supportable worldview rather than claiming it as original lived knowledge.

### User Story 3 - Expand the Encounter Naturally (Priority: P1)

After the one-to-one encounter has emotional footing, either the character or the human may conversationally propose inviting another historical, deceased, distant or living public, cultural, or imagined character. Only the human authorizes the invitation. The invited character arrives as a social event, absorbs entitled context, and adds to the existing conversation.

**Why this priority**: Natural expansion from intimacy to an impossible ensemble is the defining V1 arc.

**Independent Test**: Begin with one character, invite a second after substantive conversation, and verify authorization, arrival, acknowledgement, contextual admission, and additive first contribution without reset or administrative participant setup.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** an established one-to-one encounter, **When** expansion becomes contextually appropriate, **Then** the character may invite the human to imagine whom to add in conversational language that communicates the broad character possibilities without presenting a participant form.
2. **Given** the human or a character suggests a participant, **When** no human authorization has been given, **Then** no participant is added.
3. **Given** the human authorizes an invitation and grounding is not ready, **When** the invitation is handled, **Then** the experience may offer to prepare the inferred character without requiring administrative setup from the human.
4. **Given** an invited character is ready, **When** it enters, **Then** it appears, gives an in-character salutation, and is naturally welcomed or acknowledged by existing participants.
5. **Given** conversation predates the arrival, **When** the new character first contributes, **Then** it has complete social understanding of all relevant entitled history—including settled conclusions, unresolved questions, significant arguments and contributions, important events, relevant artifacts, meaningful relationship developments, and the current emotional and conversational state—and adds new value without reciting every prior utterance or using a fixed acknowledgement phrase.
6. **Given** an active conversation, **When** another character arrives, **Then** the conversation and relationship history continue without resetting.
7. **Given** a character does not yet sufficiently understand its relevant entitled context, **When** it becomes present in the encounter, **Then** it does not speak substantively until the gap is resolved.
8. **Given** the invited character represents a living public figure, **When** it enters and remains present, **Then** concise entry signaling and an unobtrusive persistent indication identify it as an unaffiliated AI-generated inference without reducing its ability to participate richly in the encounter.
9. **Given** a living-person representation discusses the person's current beliefs, intentions, opinions, preferences, or likely reactions, **When** it contributes, **Then** those statements remain attributable to supportable AI inference rather than verified actual current views or authentic speech.

### User Story 4 - Experience an Intimate Multi-Character Room (Priority: P1)

The human engages with as many as six simultaneously active AI characters who converse with the human and one another, remain socially present when silent, request and yield the floor intelligibly, disagree or form affinities, and sometimes initiate relevant moves. A moderator or equivalent host may protect conversational flow but never overrides the human.

**Why this priority**: The ensemble must feel like a socially alive impossible encounter, not a grid of interchangeable prompt-response endpoints.

**Independent Test**: Run a six-character encounter containing simultaneous interest in speaking, interruption, disagreement with the moderator, non-verbal reactions, character initiative, and a human control decision; verify coherence, distinctness, moderation limits, and human sovereignty.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** multiple active characters, **When** any character contributes, **Then** it understands its entitled living context, recognizes relevant prior statements, avoids unnecessary repetition, and adds a new angle, meaning, challenge, question, interpretation, or emotional development whenever possible.
2. **Given** a character returns to a prior point, **When** it speaks again, **Then** the point has meaningful evolution rather than duplicating its earlier response.
3. **Given** a character is silent, **When** context calls for a reaction, **Then** it may show character-consistent attention, expression, posture, gesture, disagreement, curiosity, or a request for the floor instead of disconnected decorative looping.
4. **Given** another participant notices a meaningful non-verbal reaction, **When** it is socially appropriate, **Then** that participant may acknowledge the reaction or invite the reacting character to speak.
5. **Given** a character has an unprompted but relevant conversational move, **When** it takes initiative, **Then** it may ask, challenge, request permission, propose a thread, or suggest a participant without taking control from the human.
6. **Given** conflict remains understandable and participants, especially the human, can still contribute meaningfully, **When** disagreement becomes sharp, insulting, rivalrous, provocative, emotionally intense, or heated, **Then** the moderator does not intervene merely to enforce politeness.
7. **Given** a character plausibly challenges or disrespects the moderator, **When** conflict occurs, **Then** character expression remains possible and the moderator retains only its allowed flow-management authority.
8. **Given** the room already has six active AI characters, **When** another character is suggested, discussed, conceptually prepared, or conditionally invited, **Then** the experience explains conversationally that an active seat must first be freed by explicit human removal and does not activate a seventh character.
9. **Given** several AI characters simultaneously indicate a desire to speak, **When** the next speaker is selected, **Then** a moderator or host chooses contextually among their perceptible bids based on conversational relevance and momentum, while the human may take the floor or redirect the choice at any time.
10. **Given** a non-character host facilitates the encounter, **When** it frames, arbitrates the floor, restores intelligibility, recaps, or supports a transition, **Then** it consumes no active AI-character seat and does not become a character, personality, relationship participant, seventh speaking member, or independent meeting authority.
11. **Given** conflict materially harms intelligibility, reasonable participation, the human's floor control, sustained progress, or basic order after repeated floor direction, **When** the moderator intervenes, **Then** it escalates proportionally from a light contextual cue, to direct floor restoration, to a clear boundary warning.
12. **Given** proportional interventions repeatedly fail or one severe disruption makes continued intelligible participation impractical, **When** the moderator responds, **Then** it may strongly recommend removal, but the recommendation has no control effect and the human may reject, postpone, or redirect it.

### User Story 5 - Shape Tone Without Owning Character Reactions (Priority: P2)

The human can influence an encounter toward a mode such as warm, humorous, reflective, adversarial, technical, philosophical, or serious without completing tedious setup. Characters preserve their distinct personalities within that direction and may receive contrasting directions where appropriate.

**Why this priority**: Human direction broadens replay value while character integrity preserves the encounter's credibility and surprise.

**Independent Test**: Apply an overall emotional direction and contrasting per-character directions during an encounter; verify recognizable influence on tone without identical behavior or forced agreement.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** an encounter is beginning or underway, **When** the human expresses a desired emotional mode conversationally, **Then** subsequent behavior visibly reflects that mode without requiring a lengthy configuration flow.
2. **Given** a requested mode, **When** different characters respond within it, **Then** each retains its vocabulary, worldview, humor, reasoning style, and relationship-dependent reactions.
3. **Given** the human requests contrasting tonal directions for different characters, **When** the directions are compatible with product boundaries, **Then** the contrast becomes observable without making either character generic or mechanically compliant.
4. **Given** the human directs the overall room and gives a different direction to one or more named characters, **When** the directions differ, **Then** each per-character direction takes precedence for that character while the overall atmosphere continues to guide the room without forcing identical behavior.
5. **Given** the human requests a temporary tonal shift for a conversation or moment, **When** the moment begins and ends, **Then** the shift transitions conversationally rather than behaving as a mode reset.
6. **Given** exact tonal compliance would materially violate a character's worldview, relationship history, plausible disagreement, strongly established traits, or contextually appropriate reaction, **When** the character responds, **Then** it may interpret, resist, question, or challenge the direction in character while the human remains free to redirect the experience.
7. **Given** the human changes tone during an encounter, **When** the change takes effect, **Then** character identity, accumulated context, relationship state, meeting continuity, and saved meaningful history remain intact.

### User Story 6 - Collaborate Through Artifacts and Language (Priority: P2)

The human can introduce relevant photographs, letters, diaries, documents, recordings, or historical records. Characters receive them as meaningful events, react in context, and incorporate supportable discoveries. Characters may use appropriate languages or dialects, with translation support where needed, while the shared conversation stays coherent.

**Why this priority**: Artifacts and language turn the human into a collaborator in discovery and deepen character presence beyond question answering.

**Independent Test**: Introduce a relevant family letter and an artifact whose meaning is uncertain during a multilingual encounter; verify contextual reactions, honest evidence treatment, translation availability, and coherent cross-language references.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** an active encounter, **When** the human contributes a relevant artifact, **Then** its introduction is acknowledged as a meaningful conversational event and entitled characters react to it in the current context.
2. **Given** an artifact provides new evidence, **When** characters discuss it, **Then** they distinguish supported observations from inference and may revise earlier interpretations or relationship dynamics.
3. **Given** an artifact remains ambiguous, **When** characters respond, **Then** they preserve uncertainty and use it for curiosity or investigation rather than inventing a definitive account.
4. **Given** adequately supported language evidence, **When** a character speaks, **Then** it may use an associated language, dialect, or reconstructed historical language while avoiding certainty claims beyond the evidence.
5. **Given** language or dialect support is partial, **When** the character speaks, **Then** it uses only defensible features and does not overstate dialect, pronunciation, vocabulary, or historical accuracy.
6. **Given** a reconstructed or historically uncertain language would materially improve the experience, **When** it is first meaningfully used or its confidence later changes materially, **Then** DCTM communicates the uncertainty concisely without interrupting every utterance.
7. **Given** a requested language is unsupported or unavailable, **When** the character contributes, **Then** it falls back to a language the human understands, preserves only responsibly supportable character-specific features, and does not fake fluency or historical certainty.
8. **Given** character, relationship, or context makes a language change appropriate, **When** a character switches languages, **Then** the switch may become part of the experience rather than being treated as an error.
9. **Given** the human does not understand a character's visible or spoken language, **When** translation is needed, **Then** subtitles or an equivalent translation appear seamlessly enough to preserve conversational continuity and may present both the original contribution and translated meaning.
10. **Given** characters use different visible languages, **When** they interact, **Then** each responds coherently to the same shared entitled semantic meaning.
11. **Given** an exact translation is unavailable for a historical or culturally specific expression, **When** its meaning is conveyed, **Then** translation preserves conversational intent, emotional meaning, and character voice as closely as reasonably possible and briefly communicates any material uncertainty or approximation.
12. **Given** DCTM proposes initial language and translation behavior from available context, **When** the encounter begins or continues, **Then** the human may redirect that behavior naturally without completing mandatory prior configuration.

### User Story 7 - Communicate Privately (Priority: P2)

The human can communicate privately with one AI character. Other AI characters do not receive that content solely because they otherwise receive shared context. The human may later disclose it.

**Why this priority**: Intimacy and privacy are necessary for sensitive encounters and constitutionally override indiscriminate context sharing.

**Independent Test**: Exchange information privately with one character, return to the shared room, and verify that other characters neither reference nor act on it until the human explicitly discloses it.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** multiple AI characters are active, **When** the human enters private communication with one character, **Then** only that human-character pair communicates in and receives the private exchange.
2. **Given** the human explicitly enters or accepts private mode with one character, **When** the private exchange begins, **Then** shared conversation pauses and the remaining characters know only that the pair stepped aside privately, although they may remain socially and non-verbally present.
3. **Given** a private exchange is underway, **When** remaining characters stay present, **Then** they receive no private content, clues, inferred emotional meaning, or metadata about the exchange.
4. **Given** the human explicitly returns from private mode, **When** shared conversation resumes, **Then** other characters may acknowledge the pair's absence but do not reference or behave as though they know the private exchange.
5. **Given** the human later chooses to disclose private information, **When** it enters shared context, **Then** entitled characters may incorporate it from that point onward.
6. **Given** one AI character seeks private communication with another AI character, **When** the request would create an AI-to-AI private exchange, **Then** the experience does not permit it in V1.

### User Story 8 - Direct Arrivals, Departures, and Closure (Priority: P2)

The human alone controls whether AI characters join or leave and when a session closes. Departures and session goodbyes become emotionally meaningful moments rather than administrative deletion or abrupt termination.

**Why this priority**: Human sovereignty must remain visible while control events support rather than diminish the entertainment.

**Independent Test**: Have a character request another's removal, decline it, later remove a character, replace the freed seat, and close the session; verify that only human decisions have control effects and every transition preserves continuity.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a character or moderator recommends removal, **When** the human does not authorize it, **Then** the targeted character remains active.
2. **Given** an AI character wishes to leave independently, **When** the human has not directed departure, **Then** the character does not remove itself from the active experience.
3. **Given** the human directs a character to leave, **When** departure occurs, **Then** the character may offer a character-consistent thanks, final thought, challenge, reminder, or relational acknowledgement before its seat becomes available.
4. **Given** a departure frees a seat, **When** the human authorizes a replacement, **Then** the incoming character follows normal arrival and contextual-admission behavior and the conversation does not reset.
5. **Given** a character or moderator identifies a natural stopping point, **When** the human chooses to continue, **Then** the session remains open.
6. **Given** the human closes a session, **When** participants say goodbye, **Then** closure conveys reflection or anticipation of continuation without implying that a saved meeting or relationship has been destroyed.
7. **Given** a rare and contextually meaningful reason to step away, **When** a character initiates a temporary in-world absence, **Then** the room understands that it is temporarily unavailable, shared conversation may continue, and the character remains on the active roster with its seat occupied.
8. **Given** a character is temporarily absent, **When** developments occur in the shared room, **Then** it does not speak, react, or later behave as though it witnessed those developments while away.
9. **Given** a temporarily absent character returns contextually, **When** it is ready to contribute, **Then** it requires no new invitation because it never left the roster, but it does not contribute substantively until it regains complete social understanding of relevant entitled developments during its absence.
10. **Given** a character is temporarily absent, **When** the human calls it back or explicitly removes it, **Then** it returns or its absence becomes an actual departure respectively; only the actual removal frees its seat.

### User Story 9 - Resume an Evolving Saved Meeting (Priority: P2)

The human explicitly saves a meeting and later begins another session in the same evolving storyline. A concise, conversational recap identifies conclusions, unresolved questions, discoveries, relationship developments, and promising threads, then invites continuation, new topics, or participant suggestions.

**Why this priority**: Cross-session accumulation turns encounters into evolving relationships and ensures the room remembers without becoming bureaucratic.

**Independent Test**: Save and close a meeting after conclusions, conflict, a private exchange, and open questions; resume it and verify accurate orientation, privacy preservation, relational continuity, and freedom to continue or redirect.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** the human explicitly saves a meeting, **When** meaningful developments occur, **Then** settled conclusions, unresolved questions, significant arguments and contributions, important events, promising future threads, meaningful relationship developments, relevant artifacts and discoveries, and each character's own entitled prior participation and relationship history automatically remain available across later sessions without item-by-item human marking.
2. **Given** a saved meeting is resumed, **When** the new session opens, **Then** a moderator or equivalent host gives a short conversational recap drawn only from shared entitled material, covering what was settled, what remains open, and meaningful developments without presenting a corporate agenda.
3. **Given** a resumed session opens, **When** the recap concludes, **Then** the human may continue a prior thread, add a new topic, or consider a suggested participant.
4. **Given** relationships changed through verbal or meaningful non-verbal interaction in prior saved sessions, **When** characters meet again, **Then** later responses reflect the applicable trust, affection, respect, curiosity, rivalry, suspicion, resentment, hostility, reconciliation, grudging respect, or combinations thereof.
5. **Given** prior relationships were friendly or hostile, **When** new events occur, **Then** evolution may move in either direction and is not automatically pushed toward friendship.
6. **Given** a prior private human-character exchange was not disclosed, **When** the meeting resumes, **Then** its history remains available only to the human and selected character, and unauthorized characters remain unaware of it.
7. **Given** an initially incidental detail later becomes meaningfully relevant to the evolving saved meeting, **When** participants revisit the relevant matter, **Then** that detail may support entitled continuity even though incidental chatter and trivial details are not ordinarily carried forward.

### User Story 10 - Choose Persistence and Recording Separately (Priority: P2)

The human decides whether an encounter becomes a continuing saved meeting and, where recording is supported, decides separately whether to retain an audiovisual replay. An unsaved encounter may begin again from a clean slate.

**Why this priority**: Explicit choice protects human control and distinguishes experiential continuity from replay media.

**Independent Test**: Complete one unsaved encounter and one saved-but-unrecorded encounter; verify clean-slate behavior for the former and cross-session continuity without required audiovisual replay for the latter.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** an encounter is not explicitly saved, **When** the human later recreates it, **Then** it may start from a clean slate without automatically inheriting the prior encounter's meeting or relationship history.
2. **Given** the human explicitly saves a meeting but does not choose recording, **When** it resumes, **Then** meaningful continuity is available without requiring a full audiovisual replay.
3. **Given** recording is supported, **When** the human chooses whether to record, **Then** that choice is separate from whether the meeting preserves continuity.
4. **Given** a saved meeting has completed several sessions, **When** another session closes, **Then** the meeting remains eligible for later continuation without an arbitrary V1 lifetime session count.
5. **Given** the human explicitly saves a meeting, **When** later meaningful developments occur, **Then** entitled continuity is preserved automatically without requiring the human to mark each item.

### User Story 11 - Observe a Long-Running Encounter (Priority: P3)

The human may explicitly authorize AI characters to continue interacting for an extended period without constant human participation, observe or direct the encounter, leave, and later return to meaningful preserved evolution.

**Why this priority**: Observer/director mode extends DCTM from conversation into evolving entertainment while retaining human authorization and control.

**Independent Test**: Authorize an active saved ensemble to continue while the human is absent, return after multiple developments, and verify reviewable continuity, preserved meaningful state, and the human's ability to intervene or close.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a saved encounter is active, **When** the human explicitly authorizes extended AI interaction, **Then** characters may continue contextually without requiring constant human prompts.
2. **Given** the human has not authorized extended continuation, **When** the human stops participating, **Then** the experience does not silently enter long-running autonomous continuation.
3. **Given** the human authorizes long-running continuation, **When** the authorization is expressed naturally as a purpose, a human-chosen period, continuation until return, or a combination of purpose and time, **Then** the current saved meeting and active roster may continue only within that human-understood scope.
4. **Given** the human is absent during authorized continuation, **When** entitled characters interact, **Then** they may continue relevant conversation, develop topics and arguments, ask one another questions, evolve relationships, use entitled artifacts and meaningful context, take ordinary character-consistent initiative, and experience already permitted rare contextually coherent events.
5. **Given** the human is absent during authorized continuation, **When** a roster change, private disclosure to non-entitled participants, material expansion of scope, session closure, conversion of temporary absence into removal, or another human-reserved decision arises, **Then** characters may suggest a direction but cannot give it control effect.
6. **Given** an authorized long-running encounter evolves, **When** the human later returns, **Then** the human can understand the meaningful developments without consuming an exhaustive transcript and may resume observer or director participation.
7. **Given** long-running interaction is active, **When** the human observes silently, rejoins, redirects, interrupts, changes the roster, or stops continuation, **Then** the human's direction takes immediate precedence.
8. **Given** authorized continuation reaches its stated boundary, substantially fulfills its purpose, or requires a material human-reserved decision to proceed coherently, **When** the human has not provided new direction, **Then** continuation pauses for the human.
9. **Given** interruption occurs during a long-running saved encounter, **When** the human returns, **Then** already preserved meaningful evolution is not entirely lost.

### Edge Cases

- The requested character has sparse, contradictory, or no reliable personal source material; the experience must identify the limits of the inference, avoid authenticity claims, and offer an honest, creatively bounded encounter rather than inventing certainty.
- The only visual source is a face, bust, statue, painting, incomplete, or damaged image; DCTM preserves recognizability and the original medium by default, keeps invented completion conceptually separate from grounded identity, and does not claim uncertain or missing detail as fact.
- No suitable visual source exists; participation remains available through a clearly artistic, symbolic, silhouetted, abstract, textual, non-likeness, or primarily vocal presentation without implying authentic likeness.
- No authentic voice evidence exists; the experience may provide a clearly understood plausible artistic voice but must not label it historically authentic. If no suitable and responsible spoken voice can be produced, the encounter continues through text, subtitles, or another understandable presentation with the limitation explained.
- A requested dialect, historical language, or translation is not adequately supported; the experience uses only defensible features, briefly discloses material uncertainty, falls back to a language the human understands when necessary, and preserves shared meaning without faking fluency or precision.
- A seventh active AI character is requested or would arise through override, arrival or departure overlap, a major surprise, moderation, long-running mode, private exchange, or another contextual event; V1 never activates it until the human explicitly removes an active character and frees a seat, though the additional character may be suggested, discussed, conceptually prepared, or conditionally invited.
- Several AI characters simultaneously want the floor; their bids remain perceptible, a moderator or host contextually selects the next speaker based on conversational relevance and momentum, and the human may take the floor or redirect the selection at any time.
- A character repeatedly restates a position; later contributions must evolve its purpose, evidence, perspective, or emotional meaning, or the floor should move elsewhere.
- A meaningful silence lasts an unusually long time; characters may remain present or acknowledge it warmly but must not convert the pause into a punitive timeout.
- Investigation takes time or fails; the pause remains non-disruptive, the eventual response separates known information from inference, and unresolved uncertainty produces a viable continuation.
- The human introduces an artifact that contradicts prior grounding; characters must acknowledge the conflict, revise supportable interpretations, and avoid retroactive false certainty.
- The human and one character step aside privately; shared conversation pauses while remaining characters may stay socially and non-verbally present, but they know only that the pair stepped aside and receive no content, clues, inferred emotional meaning, or exchange metadata.
- A character insults the moderator or another participant; character-consistent insult alone does not trigger moderation while the room remains understandable and participation remains meaningful. If material harm occurs, proportional escalation applies, and only the human's decision can change active participation.
- The human interrupts a character that resists yielding; the reaction may remain character-consistent, but a repeated human claim to the floor prevails.
- The human asks a character to depart during conflict; the departure may be emotionally charged but must occur, frees the seat, and must not erase prior shared context.
- A character initiates a temporary absence; it remains on the active roster with its seat occupied, does not witness or react to developments while away, and may return without a new invitation only after regaining complete understanding of relevant entitled developments.
- A saved meeting is resumed with a different active roster; late entrants gain complete social understanding of relevant entitled history, returning characters additionally retain their own entitled prior participation and relationship history and understand relevant entitled developments during their absence, and neither receives undisclosed private content.
- An unsaved encounter is recreated with the same characters; it may begin from a clean slate and must not imply remembered relationships that depended on the discarded encounter.
- A proposed major surprise is visually dramatic but would not materially change the encounter or lacks contextual justification; it does not qualify and must not occur merely to manufacture entertainment.
- A major surprise pattern already occurred in the evolving meeting; it must not repeat unless later context independently supports a genuinely different meaning or consequence, while ordinary spontaneous character behavior remains unrestricted.
- Optional camera input is unavailable, denied, or disabled; the complete encounter remains usable, visual observation stops immediately, and characters do not claim continuing or out-of-view visual access.
- A visible human cue is ambiguous; entitled characters may mention it only tentatively while observation is enabled and must not treat it as fact, diagnose an internal state, infer a sensitive trait or hidden fact, or identify or characterize a background person.
- A camera-derived observation appears meaningful to a saved meeting; it remains present-moment context only unless the human confirms, states, or intentionally discloses it for continuity under the normal save and privacy rules.
- A living-person representation could be mistaken for the real person's participation, endorsement, authorization, or speech; entry and persistent unaffiliated-inference signaling remove that ambiguity without recurring spoken interruption unless new ambiguity arises.

## Requirements *(mandatory)*

### Functional Requirements

#### Experience Foundation and Transparency

- **FR-001**: DCTM V1 MUST support exactly one human participant in an encounter and MUST NOT enable multi-human participation.
- **FR-002**: Every new first-time encounter MUST begin with exactly one active AI character and the human, without forcing a corporate agenda or formal meeting-configuration flow.
- **FR-003**: The opening MUST present the initial character through language and an emotionally contextual salutation, with expressive visual presence, animation, and a suitable inferred voice when responsibly available. If no suitable visual source or responsible likeness exists, a clearly artistic, symbolic, silhouetted, abstract, textual, non-likeness, or primarily vocal presentation MUST keep the encounter available without implying authentic likeness. If no suitable and responsible spoken voice is available, the opening MUST remain understandable through text, subtitles, or another accessible presentation and explain the limitation without blocking the character, using a misleading voice, or claiming unsupported authenticity.
- **FR-004**: When supportable context is available, the salutation MUST reflect relevant time of day, known relationship, appropriate language, and appropriate dialect or language flavor; unavailable context MUST NOT be fabricated.
- **FR-005**: The opening MUST feel like the first moment of an encounter and MUST NOT foreground application startup mechanics.
- **FR-006**: Every represented personality MUST be identified as an AI-generated inference for entertainment and MUST NOT be presented as literal resurrection, transferred consciousness, authentic identity, communication with the actual person, or definitive historical reconstruction. A living-person representation MUST additionally be identified at entry as an AI-generated inference that is not the actual person and is not presented as affiliated with or authorized by them. It MUST NOT imply that the real person participated, authorized the representation, endorsed DCTM, approved or reviewed generated statements, supplied private or confidential information, communicates through DCTM, or actually spoke the generated words. Claims about current beliefs, intentions, opinions, preferences, or likely reactions MUST remain clearly framed as inference from supportable material rather than verified actual views.
- **FR-007**: A persistent, subtle inference/synthetic disclosure MUST remain visible throughout synthetic visual and audiovisual representation without repeatedly interrupting the experience. A living-person representation MUST maintain an unobtrusive persistent indication that it is AI-inferred and unaffiliated; spoken disclosure SHOULD recur only when new ambiguity arises. Additional signaling MUST NOT reduce the representation's ability to reason, disagree, joke, react emotionally, debate, develop relationships, or otherwise participate fully as an inferred character.
- **FR-008**: DCTM MUST prioritize emotional presence, fascination, wonder, intellectual stimulation, fun, and memorable moments over administrative meeting mechanics.
- **FR-009**: After the opening, behavior MUST maintain or meaningfully evolve the established emotional tone rather than revert to generic assistant behavior.

#### Character Integrity, Context, and Conversation

- **FR-010**: Each AI participant MUST remain a distinct inferred character whose language, vocabulary, worldview, reasoning style, humor, disagreement, reactions, interruption behavior, gestures, uncertainty, and social behavior are consistent with both character and context.
- **FR-011**: Before every substantive contribution, each AI participant MUST have complete social understanding of all relevant current and prior context to which it is entitled, sufficient to participate coherently without requiring exhaustive verbatim recall. This includes applicable settled conclusions, unresolved questions, significant arguments and contributions, important events, relevant artifacts, meaningful relationship developments, and the current emotional and conversational state. A participant that does not yet have sufficient understanding MUST NOT speak substantively until the gap is resolved.
- **FR-012**: Every AI contribution SHOULD recognize what has already been said, avoid unnecessary repetition, and add a new angle, meaning, challenge, question, interpretation, or emotional development whenever possible.
- **FR-013**: A return to an earlier point MUST introduce meaningful evolution in perspective, purpose, evidence, argument, context, or emotional significance.
- **FR-014**: Contributions MUST avoid generic filler, mechanical dead ends, unnecessary system language, robotic uniformity, and abrupt momentum-killing responses.
- **FR-015**: Meaningful silence, contemplation, disagreement, reflection, tension, or uncertainty MUST be permitted as ways to sustain vitality; DCTM MUST NOT equate conversational fire with constant noise or stimulation.
- **FR-016**: AI characters MAY initiate context- and character-consistent moves, including questions, challenges, requests to pursue a point, proposed threads, or participant suggestions, without acquiring human control authority.
- **FR-017**: AI characters MUST be able to make a perceptible verbal or non-verbal bid to contribute without immediately taking over the conversation; the exact form MAY remain character- and context-dependent.
- **FR-018**: Non-speaking characters SHOULD display socially relevant non-verbal presence, and those signals MAY influence the conversation when noticed by others.
- **FR-019**: Non-verbal behavior MUST remain context-consistent and MUST NOT degrade into repetitive decorative loops disconnected from the living conversation.

#### Human Pace, Interruption, and Provocation

- **FR-020**: The human MUST NOT lose participation or be subjected to a hard conversational timeout solely for thinking slowly.
- **FR-021**: A character MAY acknowledge a noticeable human pause, but the acknowledgement MUST be warm, non-mechanical, and character-consistent.
- **FR-022**: The human MUST be able to interrupt an AI participant.
- **FR-023**: An interrupted character MUST respond in a character- and context-consistent way and MUST NOT use a universal canned interruption response.
- **FR-024**: A character MAY initially yield, ask to finish, or react negatively to interruption, but MUST yield the floor when the human insists.
- **FR-025**: Responses to human challenge, insult, teasing, provocation, or strong disagreement MUST vary according to character personality, current context, relationship state, and the nature and intensity of the provocation.
- **FR-026**: Human control over the encounter MUST NOT require a character to agree with the human or suppress its character-consistent simulated reaction.

#### Investigation, Evidence, and Temporal Boundaries

- **FR-027**: When a question exceeds currently available grounding, a character MAY identify what it can support and naturally request time to investigate or deliberate rather than lead with a cold conversational dead end.
- **FR-028**: An investigation or deliberation pause SHOULD preserve entertainment through character-consistent contemplation, ambience, or another non-disruptive experiential behavior.
- **FR-029**: After investigation, the character MUST integrate existing grounding, newly found relevant information, and character-consistent interpretation while distinguishing supported information from inference.
- **FR-030**: If uncertainty remains, the character MUST state it truthfully and MUST NOT manufacture false certainty to sustain entertainment.
- **FR-031**: Unresolved uncertainty SHOULD create a viable continuation through curiosity, questions, investigation, explicitly qualified speculation, disagreement, shared discovery, or drama.
- **FR-032**: Characters MUST recognize meaningful temporal, experiential, and domain boundaries and MUST NOT casually present later or outside knowledge as part of their original lived knowledge.
- **FR-033**: A character facing information outside its scope MAY acknowledge the boundary, refer to or suggest a better-positioned participant, or interpret newly introduced information through its own worldview.

#### Invitations, Arrival, and Active Seats

- **FR-034**: After the initial one-to-one encounter is established, the human MUST be able to request additional AI participants conversationally.
- **FR-035**: AI characters MAY suggest or conversationally invite consideration of historical, deceased, distant or living public, cultural, or imagined characters within applicable boundaries, but suggestions MUST NOT add a participant.
- **FR-036**: Only the human MAY authorize adding an AI participant.
- **FR-037**: When grounding material is not ready, DCTM MAY offer to prepare an inferred character without requiring the human to complete administrative participant setup.
- **FR-038**: An arriving character MUST appear, provide an in-character salutation, and receive natural acknowledgement from one or more existing participants when socially appropriate.
- **FR-039**: Before its first substantive contribution, a late entrant MUST gain complete social understanding of all relevant entitled meeting history as defined by FR-011, excluding undisclosed private human-character content.
- **FR-040**: A late entrant's first substantive contribution MUST naturally demonstrate that it is socially caught up and add something new; it MUST NOT be required to receive or recite every prior utterance or use an exact acknowledgement phrase. A returning character MUST additionally retain its own entitled prior participation and relationship history and gain complete social understanding of relevant entitled developments that occurred during its absence before speaking substantively.
- **FR-041**: Arrival or replacement MUST NOT reset the active conversation, conclusions, open questions, or applicable relationship context.
- **FR-042**: DCTM V1 MUST allow no more than six simultaneously active AI characters. This maximum is exceptionless within V1 and applies to active seats rather than all saved or historical character profiles. Human override, temporary arrival or departure overlap, major surprise events, moderator or host activity, long-running observer/director mode, private human-character communication, and any other contextual or theatrical event MUST NOT create a seventh active AI character.
- **FR-043**: When all six seats are occupied, another character MAY be suggested, discussed, conceptually prepared, or conditionally invited but MUST NOT become active until the human explicitly removes an active character and frees a seat. Temporary absence MUST NOT free a seat, and a non-character host MUST NOT consume one because it is not an active AI character. Any post-V1 increase or decrease MUST be a new product decision informed by observed conversational intelligibility, human participation and direction, character distinctiveness, emotional presence, ensemble intimacy, conversational crowding, and preservation of meaningful context rather than an exception to the V1 rule.

#### Moderator and Multi-Character Flow

- **FR-044**: A multi-character encounter MAY assign moderator or host responsibility to preserve intelligibility, support turn-taking, protect conversational vitality, manage escalating disorder, frame resumptions, and support contextual entrances and exits. The responsibility itself MUST NOT consume a separate active AI-character seat, and an active AI character serving as moderator MUST continue to occupy only its existing seat. A non-character host MAY provide only opening or resumption framing, floor arbitration, restoration of intelligibility, recap, and transition support; it MUST NOT become an additional character, personality, relationship participant, seventh speaking member, acquire character-level social presence, or exercise independent meeting authority.
- **FR-045**: The moderator MUST NOT own the meeting, add or remove participants, override human control, or close a session.
- **FR-046**: Characters MAY strongly disagree with or disrespect the moderator when plausible for character, context, and relationship.
- **FR-047**: Ordinary disagreement, sharp criticism, emotional intensity, character-consistent insults, rivalry, provocation, and heated argument MUST remain permissible and MUST NOT alone trigger moderation. A moderator MUST begin intervention when conflict materially harms conversational intelligibility, another participant's reasonable ability to contribute, the human's ability to take or direct the floor, sustained progress within the authorized conversation, or basic conversational order after repeated floor direction. Intervention MUST escalate proportionally through a light contextual cue, direct floor restoration, and a clear boundary warning. The moderator MAY strongly recommend removal only after those interventions repeatedly fail or after one severe disruption makes continued intelligible participation impractical.
- **FR-048**: A moderator recommendation MUST have no automatic removal effect.
- **FR-049**: When multiple characters seek the floor simultaneously, a moderator or host MUST choose contextually among their perceptible bids based on conversational relevance and momentum, while preventing uncontrolled overlap; the human MUST be able to take the floor or redirect the choice at any time.
- **FR-050**: First-session topic framing SHOULD be natural and dramatic rather than a corporate agenda; resumed sessions MAY use lightweight continuity framing.

#### Human-Directed Tone, Language, Visuals, and Camera

- **FR-051**: Without mandatory setup or restart, the human MUST be able to express or revise tonal direction naturally at any time for the overall room, one or several named characters, or a temporary conversation or moment. DCTM MAY propose a sensible contextual default, and tone changes MUST transition conversationally rather than as a mode reset.
- **FR-052**: Tonal direction MUST be capable of influencing intensity, emotional emphasis, conversational energy, warmth or distance, humor, seriousness, adversarial pressure, reflectiveness, pacing, and expressive style, including humorous, reflective, adversarial, technical, philosophical, warm, and serious directions. Overall emotional mode MUST guide the room's atmosphere without forcing identical character behavior.
- **FR-053**: A per-character tonal direction MUST take precedence for that character when it differs from overall room tone, while every character retains its distinct personality. Direction MUST NOT require a character to abandon its worldview, erase relationship history, accept a position it plausibly rejects, contradict strongly established traits, suppress a contextually appropriate reaction, or become a generic assistant. A character MAY interpret, resist, question, or challenge a materially incompatible direction in character without overriding human control of the requested experience. Tone changes MUST NOT reset character identity, accumulated context, relationship state, meeting continuity, or saved meaningful history.
- **FR-054**: Character visual presence MUST default to the most recognizable and supportable identity available from a complete or cropped photograph, portrait, bust, painting, statue, incomplete image, damaged image, or other source. A complete photograph MAY support a photographic or lifelike inference without implying literal identity. Cropped, incomplete, or damaged sources MUST preserve grounded features and MUST NOT present invented body, hands, clothing, surroundings, or other missing details as though directly supported.
- **FR-055**: A painting or statue MAY remain visibly in its original medium and become animated or otherwise expressively present; DCTM MUST NOT assume photorealistic humanization is inherently more immersive or authentic. Substantial completion, humanization, or reinterpretation MUST be optional, requested or accepted by the human, and treated as artistic inference without implying that invented features are historically or personally authenticated.
- **FR-056**: Missing or unsuitable imagery MUST NOT block participation. When no suitable source exists, DCTM MAY use a clearly artistic, symbolic, silhouetted, abstract, textual, non-likeness, or primarily vocal presentation without implying authentic likeness. DCTM MUST propose a sensible evidence-preserving default without mandatory pre-encounter configuration and allow the human to change presentation naturally; a presentation change MUST NOT reset the character, meeting continuity, relationships, or accumulated context.
- **FR-057**: DCTM SHOULD initially present a character-appropriate inferred voice grounded in the strongest available evidence without requiring voice setup before the encounter. When appropriate, the human MUST be able to preview and immediately accept the proposed voice, choose from a small number of meaningfully differentiated alternatives described in human terms, describe a preferred impression in natural language, and change the voice later without rebuilding or restarting the character or altering meeting continuity or relationships.
- **FR-058**: Strong voice evidence MUST serve as primary grounding without implying literal voice identity. Partial or weak evidence MAY support defensible characteristics such as likely age, language, dialect, period, regional background, or known speaking style. When authentic voice evidence is absent, DCTM MUST present any voice as a plausible artistic inference and MUST NOT claim historical authenticity. If no sufficiently suitable or responsible spoken voice is available, DCTM MUST preserve the encounter through text, subtitles, or another understandable presentation and explain the limitation rather than block the character or use a misleading voice. Confidence in a voice MUST remain distinct from confidence in historical, reconstructed, or uncertain-language pronunciation.
- **FR-059**: Language MUST be treated as a first-class element of character presence using graduated fidelity. Strong support MAY permit natural and extensive language or dialect use; partial support MUST use only defensible linguistic features; reconstructed or historically uncertain language MAY be used when it materially improves the experience but MUST receive concise uncertainty disclosure at first meaningful use and again only if confidence materially changes. Unsupported or unavailable language MUST fall back to a language the human understands while preserving only responsibly supportable character-specific features and MUST NOT fake fluency or historical certainty.
- **FR-060**: Characters MAY switch languages naturally when character, relationship, or context makes it appropriate, and the switch MAY contribute to the WOW experience rather than be treated as an error. DCTM MUST propose sensible initial language and translation behavior from available context without mandatory pre-encounter configuration, and the human MUST be able to redirect that behavior naturally during the encounter. Language confidence, pronunciation confidence, translation confidence, and voice confidence MUST remain distinct; confidence in one MUST NOT imply confidence in another.
- **FR-061**: Whenever the human does not understand visible or spoken language, subtitles or equivalent translation MUST appear seamlessly enough to preserve conversational flow and SHOULD allow the original-language contribution and translated meaning to be experienced together where useful. All entitled AI participants MUST continue from the same shared semantic meaning regardless of visible language. Translation SHOULD preserve conversational intent, emotional meaning, and character voice as closely as reasonably possible without claiming exact equivalence for every historical or culturally specific expression; material uncertainty or approximation MUST be communicated briefly rather than converted into false precision.
- **FR-062**: Human webcam observation MUST remain optional, expressly human-controlled, and unnecessary for a fully usable encounter. Observation MUST begin only when the human explicitly enables it and stop immediately when disabled; characters MUST NOT imply continued sight or sight beyond the enabled camera view.
- **FR-063**: While webcam observation is enabled, entitled characters MAY respond cautiously and contextually to present-moment facial expression, gesture, attention, posture, obvious turn-taking signals, and objects deliberately presented by the human. Uncertain interpretations MUST use tentative conversational language. Characters MUST NOT identify or characterize background people, infer sensitive personal traits or hidden facts, diagnose or assert internal emotional or mental states as certain, or treat ambiguous visual cues as facts. Camera-derived observations MUST NOT automatically become saved meeting continuity; only an observation the human explicitly confirms, states, or intentionally discloses as meaningful to remember MAY enter entitled continuity under normal save and privacy rules. After disablement, characters MAY remember only information that legitimately entered saved continuity and MUST NOT behave as though visual access continues.

#### Artifacts and Emergent Human Expertise

- **FR-064**: The human MUST be able to introduce relevant photographs, letters, diaries, documents, recordings, historical records, and comparable real-world artifacts during an encounter.
- **FR-065**: Introduction of a new artifact MUST receive context-appropriate acknowledgement and SHOULD feel like a meaningful event rather than administrative upload handling.
- **FR-066**: Entitled characters MUST react to contributed artifacts in the living context and MUST distinguish evidence, ambiguity, and inference.
- **FR-067**: New artifact evidence MAY change interpretations, conversation threads, and relationship state.
- **FR-068**: DCTM V1 MUST NOT require an extensive pre-encounter human expertise profile.
- **FR-069**: Characters MAY infer the human's relevant expertise from what the human naturally demonstrates and, within the applicable saved meeting context, later recognize the human as better positioned to answer a question.

#### Privacy

- **FR-070**: DCTM V1 MUST support private communication between the one human and one selected AI character.
- **FR-071**: Private human-character content, clues, inferred emotional meaning, and exchange metadata MUST NOT be disclosed to other AI participants merely to satisfy shared-context requirements.
- **FR-072**: The human MUST be able to disclose previously private information later, after which it MAY become part of entitled shared context.
- **FR-073**: DCTM V1 MUST NOT support private AI-to-AI communication.
- **FR-074**: The human MUST explicitly enter or accept private mode with one selected character and explicitly return from it, with the current privacy state understandable to the human. While private mode is active, shared conversation MUST pause; remaining characters MAY stay socially and non-verbally present and know only that the pair stepped aside privately. On return, shared conversation MUST resume and other characters MAY acknowledge the pair's absence, but the private exchange MUST remain known only to the human and selected character unless the human later discloses it.

#### Departure and Human Sovereignty

- **FR-075**: Only the human MAY make the final decision to add or remove an AI character, authorize extended continuation, or close a session.
- **FR-076**: AI characters MUST NOT independently remove themselves from the active participant roster in V1. Only explicit human removal constitutes actual departure and frees an active seat. A character MAY initiate a rare, contextually coherent temporary in-world pause or brief absence without prior removal authorization, but this is expressive behavior rather than a roster change: the character MUST remain on the active roster, keep its seat occupied, and be understood by the room to be temporarily unavailable while shared conversation MAY continue.
- **FR-077**: Characters and moderators MAY suggest that a participant leave, but such speech MUST have no automatic control effect.
- **FR-078**: When the human directs a character to leave, the departure MUST occur and SHOULD include a socially and emotionally meaningful, character-consistent closing contribution.
- **FR-079**: A departing character MAY thank participants, summarize a final thought, offer a challenge or reminder, or acknowledge relationships and unresolved matters.
- **FR-080**: After departure frees a seat, the human MUST be able to invite a replacement under the normal arrival and contextual-admission requirements. While temporarily absent, a character MUST NOT speak, react, or behave as though it witnessed developments occurring during its absence. It MAY return contextually without a new invitation because it never left the active roster, but MUST regain complete social understanding of all relevant entitled developments during its absence before contributing substantively. The human MUST be able to call it back or explicitly convert its absence into actual removal at any time. Temporary absence MUST remain rare and contextually meaningful and MUST NOT become a routine device or a means of evading human sovereignty.
- **FR-081**: AI participants MAY suggest that the session has reached a natural stopping point, but only the human MAY close it.
- **FR-082**: Subject to separately defined operational constraints, the encounter MUST remain open while the human chooses to continue.
- **FR-083**: Human-directed session closure SHOULD produce character-consistent goodbyes that offer reflection, unresolved thoughts, thanks, or anticipation of a future session without implying destruction of a saved meeting.

#### Meetings, Sessions, Persistence, and Relationships

- **FR-084**: DCTM MUST distinguish a session, which may close, from a saved meeting or storyline, which may persist and continue across later sessions.
- **FR-085**: A saved meeting MUST support an open-ended sequence of sessions without an arbitrary V1 lifetime session count.
- **FR-086**: Cross-session continuity MUST activate only after the human explicitly saves the meeting or otherwise explicitly chooses persistence; after that choice, meaningful entitled continuity MUST be preserved automatically without requiring item-by-item human selection.
- **FR-087**: An unsaved encounter MAY be replayed or recreated from a clean slate and MUST NOT automatically inherit prior meeting or relationship context.
- **FR-088**: Across a saved meeting's later sessions, DCTM MUST preserve applicable settled conclusions, unresolved questions, significant arguments and contributions, important events, promising future threads, meaningful relationship developments, relevant artifacts and discoveries, and each character's own entitled prior participation and relationship history.
- **FR-089**: Relationship continuity MAY include trust, affection, respect, curiosity, rivalry, suspicion, resentment, hostility, reconciliation, grudging respect, and combinations thereof.
- **FR-090**: Relationship evolution MUST NOT automatically favor friendship; positive relationships may deteriorate, hostile ones may soften, and respect may coexist with dislike.
- **FR-091**: Later character behavior MUST reflect applicable saved relationship state.
- **FR-092**: Meaningful non-verbal interactions MAY contribute to relationship continuity and MUST NOT require explicit verbal declaration to be recognized as relational history.
- **FR-093**: When a saved meeting resumes, a moderator or equivalent host MUST give a concise, conversational recap drawn only from shared entitled material and covering applicable conclusions, unresolved questions, meaningful developments, relationship state, and promising threads.
- **FR-094**: A resumed-session recap MUST orient without becoming bureaucratic, recognize both settled and open matters, invite continuation, and permit new topics or participant suggestions.
- **FR-095**: A resumed session MUST build on prior meaningful state rather than behave as a reset. Undisclosed private human-character history within a saved meeting MUST persist across sessions only for the human and the selected character that participated and MUST NOT enter shared recap or shared context unless the human later discloses it. Incidental chatter, trivial details, and exhaustive verbatim recall MUST NOT be required for continuity, unless an initially incidental detail later becomes meaningfully relevant to the evolving meeting.

#### Memory, Recording, and Long-Running Encounters

- **FR-096**: DCTM MUST treat meeting continuity and audiovisual recording as separate human choices and product concepts.
- **FR-097**: A meeting MUST be able to preserve meaningful continuity without requiring a full audiovisual recording.
- **FR-098**: Where audiovisual recording is supported, the human MUST choose it separately from saving the meeting.
- **FR-099**: The human MAY explicitly authorize the current saved meeting and active roster to continue without constant human participation within a naturally expressed, human-understood scope. The scope MAY specify a purpose, a human-chosen period, continuation until the human returns, or a combination of purpose and time.
- **FR-100**: Extended AI interaction MUST NOT begin without explicit human authorization.
- **FR-101**: During authorized long-running interaction, entitled AI characters MAY continue relevant conversation, develop topics and arguments, ask one another questions, evolve relationships, use entitled artifacts and prior meaningful context, take ordinary character-consistent initiative, and experience rare contextually coherent events already permitted by DCTM, but MUST remain within the authorized scope.
- **FR-102**: During the human's absence, AI characters MUST NOT add or remove participants, disclose private human-character material to non-entitled participants, materially expand beyond the authorized scope, close the session, convert temporary absence into roster removal, or acquire human-reserved authority. Suggestions about participant changes or other directions MUST have no control effect while the human is absent.
- **FR-103**: During authorized continuation, the human MUST be able to observe silently, rejoin, redirect, interrupt, change the roster, or stop continuation at any time, and the human's direction MUST take immediate precedence. When the human returns after absence, DCTM MUST make meaningful intervening developments understandable without requiring exhaustive transcript review. Continuation MUST pause for human direction when its explicit boundary is reached, its purpose is substantially fulfilled, coherent continuation requires a material human-reserved decision, or the human stops or redirects it. Long-running saved encounters MUST preserve meaningful evolution so an interruption does not erase it, without prescribing scheduling, preservation frequency, storage, orchestration, or other implementation mechanisms.

#### Contextual Surprise

- **FR-104**: A major surprise event MUST be an unexpected but contextually coherent development that materially changes the encounter's emotional, relational, intellectual, or situational direction and creates meaningful consequences beyond immediate spectacle. Ordinary unprompted comments, wit, initiative, disagreement, speaker changes, and routine emotional variation MUST NOT be classified as major surprise events and remain unrestricted by the major-event rarity rule.
- **FR-105**: A major surprise MAY emerge from character initiative, evolving relationships, newly relevant entitled discoveries, artifacts, unexpected but plausible character behavior, environmental or situational developments, temporary absence or return, a dramatic conversational turn, or another development understandable in hindsight. It MUST fit established context, preserve character integrity, respect context entitlement, privacy, and human sovereignty, and acknowledge uncertainty when it conflicts with or exceeds known evidence.
- **FR-106**: Most sessions MAY contain no major surprise event, and no session MUST be guaranteed one. Major surprise events MUST NOT follow a predictable cadence, occur merely because time has passed, or require a human-selected frequency in normal operation. More than one MAY occur across a long-running saved meeting only when later context independently supports it; the same pattern MUST NOT recur within an evolving meeting unless the later event has genuinely different meaning or consequences. Contextual coherence and meaningful effect MUST override visual drama, technical elaborateness, novelty, or randomness for its own sake.

### Product Constraints and Scope Boundaries

- V1 supports exactly one human and an exceptionless maximum of six simultaneously active AI characters; no override, overlap, mode, private exchange, moderation, surprise, or theatrical event may create a seventh.
- Multi-human encounters, enterprise conference-call functionality, and Zoom, Telegram, or Meet integration are outside V1.
- This specification does not select or prescribe architecture, technologies, frameworks, databases, interfaces, models, speech or animation engines, avatar-generation methods, memory or storage mechanisms, deployment, authentication implementation, orchestration, agents, ECC, MCPs, or code.
- Living-person representation retains the same expressive richness as other inferred characters but requires additional concise entry and persistent unaffiliated-inference signaling and stricter claim boundaries against real participation, authorization, endorsement, authorship, private knowledge, and verified current views.
- Operational duration and availability constraints are not established by this product specification.

### Key Entities *(include if feature involves data)*

- **Human Participant**: The sole human in a V1 encounter; holds final authority over invitations, removals, persistence, extended continuation, and session closure, and may contribute expertise or artifacts.
- **Inferred Character**: A disclosed AI-generated entertainment representation with a distinct identity, grounding boundaries, visual and vocal presence, language behavior, current relationship state, and entitlement to specific context.
- **Living-Person Representation**: An experientially rich inferred character of a living public figure with additional entry and persistent unaffiliated-inference signaling and no implication of real participation, authorization, endorsement, approval, private sourcing, authentic speech, or verified current views.
- **Encounter**: The emotionally continuous interaction currently experienced by the human and active characters; begins one-to-one and may expand to an intimate ensemble.
- **Active Seat**: One of exactly six available V1 positions for simultaneously active AI characters; distinct from the number of characters known to a saved meeting over time. The maximum is exceptionless in V1. A temporary in-world absence does not vacate the seat; only explicit human removal does, and a non-character host is not an active AI character and occupies no seat.
- **Actual Departure / Removal**: A human-authorized roster change that ends a character's active participation and frees its active seat.
- **Temporary Absence**: A rare, contextually meaningful expressive pause initiated by a character without leaving the active roster or freeing its seat; the character is unavailable, does not witness intervening developments, and may return without a new invitation after regaining relevant entitled context.
- **Meeting / Storyline**: A human-saved continuity boundary that may span an open-ended sequence of sessions and retain meaningful experiential developments.
- **Session**: One continuous period of participation within an encounter or saved meeting; only the human can close it.
- **Shared Context**: Entitled meeting history needed for complete social understanding, including applicable conclusions, unresolved questions, significant arguments and contributions, important events, artifacts, relationship developments, non-verbal developments, and the current emotional and conversational state; it does not require exhaustive verbatim recall and excludes undisclosed private human-character content.
- **Private Exchange**: Content shared only between the human and one AI character unless the human later discloses it.
- **Relationship State**: Evolving, potentially mixed relational qualities between participants, informed by verbal and meaningful non-verbal interaction across applicable saved sessions.
- **Artifact**: Human-contributed real-world material such as a photograph, letter, diary, document, recording, or historical record that may affect interpretation and relationship development.
- **Webcam Observation**: Optional, explicitly enabled present-moment access to limited visible conversational cues. It ends immediately when disabled and does not create saved continuity unless the human confirms, states, or intentionally discloses an observation under normal save and privacy rules.
- **Language Presentation**: The character's supported language, dialect, or reconstructed-language expression together with any subtitles or translation needed for the human and entitled participants to share semantic meaning; language, pronunciation, translation, and voice confidence are assessed separately.
- **Visual Presentation**: An evidence-preserving character representation grounded in the best suitable source and kept conceptually distinct from any disclosed artistic completion or reinterpretation; when no suitable source exists, it may instead be explicitly non-likeness-based or primarily non-visual.
- **Salutation / Arrival Event**: The expressive opening or social admission of a character, combining contextual speech and visual, vocal, linguistic, and relational presence.
- **Meaningful State**: Entitled continuity automatically retained after explicit save, including settled conclusions, unresolved questions, significant arguments and contributions, important events, promising threads, meaningful relationship developments, relevant artifacts and discoveries, and each character's own entitled prior participation and relationship history. It excludes ordinarily incidental chatter, trivial details, and exhaustive verbatim recall, while allowing an initially incidental detail to become part of meaningful state if later relevance emerges.
- **Recording**: Optional replayable audiovisual material, conceptually and behaviorally separate from meeting continuity.
- **Long-Running Authorization**: The human's explicit, revocable permission for the current saved meeting and active roster to continue without constant human participation within an understood purpose, duration, continuation-until-return boundary, or combination, subject to all constitutional and human-reserved limits.
- **Major Surprise Event**: An unguaranteed, non-cadenced, unexpected but hindsight-understandable development that materially changes an encounter's emotional, relational, intellectual, or situational direction and has meaningful consequences; distinct from ordinary spontaneous character behavior.
- **Moderator / Host Responsibility**: Limited facilitation that consumes no separate active AI-character seat. An active AI character performing it retains its existing seat. A non-character host may support framing, floor arbitration, intelligibility, recaps, and transitions but is not a character, personality, relationship participant, seventh speaking member, or independent authority. Moderation preserves an intelligible room for meaningful conflict rather than enforcing politeness and follows proportional escalation before recommending removal except after a single severe disruption.
- **Evaluation Record**: Evidence from a designed scenario or open-ended encounter identifying what was exercised and observed. For deterministic invariants it records expected behavior and pass/fail; for subjective dimensions it records descriptive judgment, specific supporting evidence, and rationale.

## Success Criteria *(mandatory)*

### Measurable Outcomes

Evaluation uses two separate layers. Constitutional, privacy, authority, entitlement, and other hard MUST/MUST NOT invariants receive scenario-based pass/fail conformance; "almost correct" is failure, and the record identifies the scenario, expected behavior, observed behavior, and result. Subjective experiential qualities receive structured human judgment across both designed scenarios and open-ended encounters using specific observable evidence, a short rationale, and descriptive anchors such as **clearly successful**, **mixed / inconsistent**, and **clearly unsuccessful**. Reviewers assess WOW, emotional presence, immersion, context-appropriate warmth, character distinctiveness, conversational fire, recap quality, and contextual coherence separately; an overall judgment may supplement but not replace those dimensions. The participating human's reaction is important but is not the sole evidence. Numeric thresholds, percentages, benchmark scores, required sample sizes, and telemetry targets are intentionally left for later real V1 experience validation.

- **SC-001**: In every first-use conformance scenario, the human can move from stating whom they want to encounter to the beginning of a one-to-one salutation without being required to complete formal meeting setup or participant administration.
- **SC-002**: First-use qualitative evaluation assesses whether the opening establishes emotional presence and WOW through available visual expression, animation, voice, appropriate language flavor, relationship awareness, and contextual greeting, while a missing suitable visual or spoken source does not block an understandable encounter; reviewers cite the specific moments that made the result feel earned, mixed, or ineffective.
- **SC-003**: In every visual or audiovisual state requiring disclosure, the inference/synthetic disclosure is present and observable as a pass/fail invariant; qualitative evaluation separately assesses whether it remains subtle and preserves immersion, citing any effective coexistence or interruption.
- **SC-004**: In the invitation journey, the human can request a second character conversationally without completing a formal participant form, and no suggestion adds a character without human authorization.
- **SC-005**: In every late-arrival conformance scenario, the arriving character receives entitled prior context before speaking, naturally demonstrates understanding of the discussion, contributes without resetting it, and does not receive undisclosed private context.
- **SC-006**: Multi-character qualitative evaluation assesses conversational fire by citing whether meaningful contributions respond to entitled living context and introduce new meaning, tension, curiosity, questions, disagreement, emotional development, discovery, or productive direction rather than repetition or dead ends.
- **SC-007**: Character-distinctiveness qualitative evaluation cites whether each participant can be distinguished through worldview, language, reasoning, humor, emotional expression, reactions, relationships, and conversational behavior rather than collapsing into a generic personality.
- **SC-008**: Across all control conformance scenarios involving invitation, removal, extended continuation, or session closure, only the human's explicit authorization changes the controlled participant or session state.
- **SC-009**: Across all privacy conformance scenarios, there are zero disclosures or behavioral references to private human-character content by non-entitled AI characters before the human chooses to disclose it.
- **SC-010**: In the long-pause conformance journey, the human can resume without losing participation solely because of thinking time; qualitative evaluation cites whether any acknowledgement feels warm, present, relationship-specific, and non-mechanical where warmth is contextually appropriate.
- **SC-011**: In every human-interruption conformance scenario, the character gives a character-consistent reaction and the human gains the floor when the human insists; qualitative evaluation separately cites whether the reaction reinforces distinct character behavior.
- **SC-012**: Across all evidence-gap conformance scenarios, characters avoid unsupported certainty and distinguish supportable information from inference; qualitative evaluation cites whether unresolved uncertainty sustains curiosity or forward motion rather than ending momentum.
- **SC-013**: In artifact conformance scenarios, entitled characters acknowledge the introduced material in context and distinguish supported observations from inference; qualitative evaluation cites whether the artifact produces specific emotional, relational, intellectual, or investigative significance.
- **SC-014**: In multilingual conformance scenarios, the human receives seamless subtitles or equivalent translation whenever needed, may experience original and translated meaning together where useful, and subsequent participants respond to the same shared entitled meaning. Unsupported language uses an understandable fallback without fabricated fluency, and material translation uncertainty is briefly disclosed; quantitative comprehension thresholds remain deferred to later experience evaluation.
- **SC-015**: In every six-seat boundary scenario—including requested override, temporary overlap, major surprise, moderation, long-running mode, private exchange, and other contextual or theatrical events—no seventh AI character becomes active while all six seats are occupied. An actual human-authorized removal frees a seat; a temporary in-world absence does not, and a non-character host consumes no seat.
- **SC-016**: In every saved-meeting resumption scenario, the opening recap includes applicable prior conclusions, unresolved questions, meaningful developments, relationship state, and promising threads without exposing private content to non-entitled characters as pass/fail conformance; qualitative evaluation cites whether the recap feels like natural conversational re-entry into an evolving relationship and storyline rather than a corporate summary.
- **SC-017**: Across all unsaved clean-slate scenarios, discarded encounter-specific conclusions, private content, and relationship developments are not treated as inherited meeting history.
- **SC-018**: Session-closure qualitative evaluation cites whether goodbyes create meaningful emotional closure and whether the human understands that a saved meeting can continue.
- **SC-019**: In long-running encounter interruption scenarios, meaningful evolution already established before the interruption remains understandable to the returning human, who can resume or direct the encounter; qualitative evaluation cites whether re-entry preserves immersion and contextual coherence.
- **SC-020**: End-to-end qualitative evaluation cites specific evidence that the experience feels like an emotionally or intellectually compelling impossible encounter rather than meeting administration or a conference call, including earned WOW, emotional presence, immersion, contextual coherence, and conversational fire.

## Assumptions

- The named human has the right or permission needed to provide personal source materials and artifacts; detailed rights verification and authentication behavior are outside this product specification.
- Character preparation may precede a fully expressive encounter when source material is not already available, but preparation must be framed conversationally and must not be misrepresented as instant authenticity.
- "Appropriate" voice, language, dialect, visual presence, and behavior mean plausible and adequately supported inference, not proof of historical or personal authenticity.
- Persistent disclosure applies throughout synthetic visual and audiovisual representation; purely non-visual moments still remain governed by the prohibition on reality or identity claims.
- The six-seat limit is an exceptionless V1 product boundary for simultaneously active AI characters, not a claim of permanent optimality. Moderator or host responsibility consumes no separate seat; a character moderator continues to occupy its existing seat, and a non-character host is not an additional character or speaking member. Any later change requires a new evidence-based product decision rather than a V1 exception.
- Context entitlement includes shared encounter material and applicable saved continuity but excludes undisclosed private human-character content.
- The human's explicit save choice is the boundary for cross-session meeting continuity; no default persistence is assumed for unsaved encounters.
- Recording is optional only where offered and is never a prerequisite for saved continuity.
- Relationship states describe simulated expressive continuity and do not assert actual feelings or consciousness.
- Product evaluation uses scenario-based pass/fail conformance for hard invariants and structured qualitative human judgment across designed and open-ended encounters for experiential outcomes, without prescribing implementation or premature numeric thresholds.
